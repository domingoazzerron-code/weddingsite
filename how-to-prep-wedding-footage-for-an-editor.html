Guide for wedding videographers

How to prep wedding footage for an editor

A clean handoff is the fastest way to a better first draft. Here is what an editor needs from you, in the order it matters.

By Marcus Wedding Studio · Updated November 2026

1. Back up first, then organize

Copy every card to two places before you send anything, and keep the backup until the project is delivered. Then create one folder per camera or angle and keep the original file names. Editors sync by timecode and file order, so renaming clips can cause problems.

2. Label your audio

Include every audio source: lavalier mics, recorders and anything captured at the ceremony, vows and speeches. Note which file belongs to whom and where. Good audio is often what decides whether a speech can be used in the highlight film.

3. Separate drone and b-roll

Put drone shots, details and b-roll in their own folders. It helps the editor find cutaways quickly, and it keeps the main camera folders clean.

4. Share a link that works

Use Dropbox, Google Drive or a similar service. Open the link in a private window to check that it works without your login. Tell your editor the total size and the number of cameras, and if your files are large or messy, say so, so you can agree on the easiest way to send them.

5. Write a short note about the wedding

  • Couple’s names, the wedding date and a rough timeline of the day.
  • Must-have moments: vows, first kiss, toasts, entrances and special details.
  • Names and pronunciations of key people, and any cultural or religious ceremony notes.
  • Anything to leave out.

6. Send style references

Two or three of your own films, with a line about what you like in each, tell an editor more than a page of adjectives. Mention the editing style you want (cinematic, documentary or matching your existing work), the length of each deliverable and the aspect ratios you need, for example 16:9 and vertical 9:16.

7. Sort out the music

Share the genre and mood you want. Commercial songs are copyrighted and need a license to be used in a final film. If you have your own music account, such as Musicbed or Artlist, send the track links. If not, ask your editor to provide licensed music in a similar style. Songs you list still help set the mood and pacing.

8. Give a real deadline

Tell your editor the date you need the film and whether it’s firm. If you need it faster than the usual turnaround, say so on day one, because rush work needs planning.
